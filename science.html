Section 02 • Pathophysiological Continuum & Asymptomatic Imaging

The MRI Paradox: High Prevalence of Disc Degeneration in Pain-Free Populations

Decades of magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) research have revealed a profound diagnostic disconnect: structural spine pathology is ubiquitous in asymptomatic individuals. In their landmark systematic review of 3,110 asymptomatic subjects, Brinjikji et al. (2015) demonstrated that intervertebral disc degeneration increases linearly with age, appearing in 37% of 20-year-olds, 52% of 40-year-olds, and up to 96% of 80-year-olds—even among individuals with zero history of back pain. Disc bulge, loss of disc height, and facet arthrosis follow identical trajectories.

Earlier foundational studies by Jensen et al. (1994) similarly found that 64% of pain-free adults had abnormal disc contours on MRI, with 27% exhibiting frank disc protrusions or extrusions. These findings establish that morphological "abnormalities" on static imaging are normal anatomical variants of spinal aging rather than isolated surgical pain generators.
